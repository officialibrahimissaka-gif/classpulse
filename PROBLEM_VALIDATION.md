## What “painful and expensive” means

Measure a real event, its recurrence, and the part preventable by the proposed workflow. Use the customer's own loaded hourly costs and contractual terms. No amounts have been supplied.

- **Payroll administration:** observed rework hours × customer-supplied loaded labor cost, plus documented external correction fees. Do not add the same employee time twice.
- **Placement failure:** permanently lost net contribution attributable to a preventable failure, plus incremental replacement work. Do not equate the whole placement fee or salary with loss.
- **Start delay:** separate delayed cash collection, genuinely lost billable capacity, and unrecoverable contribution. Include only the share causally attributable to coordination.
- **Staffing invoice disputes:** documented write-offs and incremental collection/reconciliation effort. Cash timing and revenue loss must remain separate.
- **Risk exposure:** record actual incidents and verified contractual/regulatory exposure separately. Do not turn hypothetical maximum penalties into expected savings.

For each calculation record currency, period, denominator, source, preventable share and uncertainty. Present a range if inputs are estimated. Compare net benefit with subscription, integration, training, worker effort and ongoing administration. A large gross problem does not guarantee a viable software margin.

## Evidence ladder

1. **Opinion:** “This sounds useful.” No validated need.
2. **Specific recent incident:** a credible mechanism, still potentially exceptional.
3. **Corroborated recurring workflow:** artifacts or observation across completed cycles establish local recurrence.
4. **Buying evidence:** named owner, decision process, current spending, accepted evaluation terms; a nonbinding letter remains weaker than payment.
5. **Paid repeatable value:** collected payment, agreed outcome improvement, continued use or renewal, and acceptable delivery cost across independent customers.

Current status is before level 2 for every hypothesis. Do not promote a claim because it sounds plausible or because a vendor markets a related category.

## Decision rules

**[E] Continue discovery** when recent incidents recur but cost, buyer, alternatives or access is unclear. **Narrow or pivot** when the pain exists only in one operational model, industry, or volume range. **Stop the wedge** when existing tools solve it cheaply, customers cannot change the process, or the likely solution requires an unplanned licensed service or full-system replacement.

Do not average away a fatal dependency. Failure to obtain lawful data access, a usable integration, required participant adoption, or a clearly authorized role can disqualify a higher-scoring idea. Before any build, require the progression evidence in [CUSTOMER_INTERVIEW_PLAN.md](CUSTOMER_INTERVIEW_PLAN.md), live competitive verification, and applicable regulatory review.
