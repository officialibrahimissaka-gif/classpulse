## How to read this comparison

The table uses **[B] historical category positioning** to build a diligence shortlist. It is not a verified current feature inventory. Strengths are potential reasons buyers select that category; weaknesses are **[H] tradeoffs to test**, not documented customer complaints. “Verify” means unknown, not absent. No numeric vendor price or current quote-only policy is asserted.

Do not rank vendors by feature count until country support, plan entitlement, implementation responsibilities, and actual workflow outcomes are demonstrated. “Payroll” can mean calculation, export, managed service, payment, or coordination; these are different capabilities.

## Non-software and embedded substitutes

**[H]** Shared spreadsheets, email, messaging, outsourced payroll, an accountant, a PRO/mobility provider, and existing ATS custom fields may be cheaper or more trusted than another product. A proposed workflow must outperform this combination after data entry, integration, training, and vendor management costs.

Ask buyers what they would stop paying for or stop doing. If the answer is “nothing,” the new product needs enough incremental commercial benefit to justify an additional budget line. Do not describe spreadsheets as inherently broken; obtain the actual failure case.

## Comparable pricing collection

No current prices are verified. Collect written, dated quotes or captured official pricing for three **[E] standardized comparison cases**:

1. Employer with 100 employees, one UAE entity, three locations, payroll-input approvals and attendance import.
2. Recruitment agency with 10 internal users, an existing ATS, two external client approvers, and 40 accepted placements per month.
3. Group with 300 workers across three entities in two countries, local payroll providers retained.

These are comparison assumptions, not market averages. Specify currency, tax treatment, term, billing period, minimum users, active versus stored workers, implementation, migration, training, support, integrations/API, storage, messaging, document signing, sandbox, exit/export fees, and price changes at renewal. Identify required modules and responsibility for localization updates.

Normalize to first-year and recurring annual total cost at the same scope. For service providers, separate software/service fees from employment costs, government charges, insurance, deposits and money held for payroll. Convert currency only with a dated exchange-rate source. Do not calculate savings before incumbent costs and switching effort are known.

## Onboarding comparison protocol

**[U] No onboarding experience has been tested.** Public “book a demo” or trial claims would not establish implementation duration or success.

Give each shortlisted vendor the same redacted sample and script: import 20 synthetic records, set up one entity, configure a real approval chain, invite an external stakeholder, simulate an exception, export the result, and request full data export. Use no real employee documents during evaluation.

Record elapsed time and human effort separately for purchase-to-access, first import, permissions, workflow configuration, integrations, first useful output, and recovery from a mistake. Record who performs each step, what training is required, support response, localization ownership, migration cost, and exit path. Check whether approvals and audit history survive exports. A sales demonstration is not a hands-on implementation result.

## Competitive conclusion

**[H]** The most defensible initial differentiation may be a demonstrably better handoff between agencies, clients, and workers, rather than another candidate database or generic HR record. This survives only if native ATS configuration and existing onboarding products fail the same test at comparable total cost. That is the next competitive question to answer, not an established whitespace finding.
