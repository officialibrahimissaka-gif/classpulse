# Customer interview and observation plan

Prepared: 1 November 2026. **Proposed research; no interviews, commitments or purchases have occurred.** Counts, timing and gates are **[E] planning choices**, not statistically representative sample sizes. See [market evidence definitions](MARKET_RESEARCH.md).

## Decisions this research must support

Choose whether any of three workflows merits an MVP: agency placement-to-start, employer payroll-input exceptions, or international-hiring document coordination. Establish the actual owner, recent costly event, existing alternative, accessible budget, and implementation constraints. A participant saying an idea is useful is insufficient.

## Recruitment and sample

Target **24 organizations** over approximately four weeks, subject to access. Record organizations separately from individual interviews so several colleagues do not masquerade as independent demand.

| Primary organization stratum | Target [E] | Required variation |
|---|---:|---|
| Permanent recruitment agencies | 6 | Small and larger teams; retained/contingency models; different ATS; include satisfied incumbents |
| Temporary staffing / workforce suppliers | 4 | Different assignment duration, client approval process, payroll and invoicing ownership |
| UAE direct employers | 10 | At least one each in construction, hospitality, retail, logistics, healthcare and professional services; remaining four fill underrepresented size/site/tool profiles |
| Cross-border employer groups | 4 | UAE plus another country; different legal entities and payroll-provider models |

Classify each organization once in the primary count, with overlapping tags for industry, international hiring, SME/mid-market size, jurisdiction, headcount, locations and systems. Balance owner-led and HR-led buying, spreadsheet-heavy and suite-heavy workflows, and organizations that report no problem. Do not infer UAE-wide prevalence from this purposive sample.

Within the 24 organizations, seek **[E] 8 follow-up conversations** with payroll staff, recruiters, line managers or HR administrators and **6 voluntary employee/candidate conversations** where safe and appropriate. Interview economic buyer and operator separately where possible. Ask employers for opt-in invitations; do not pressure employees to participate or disclose immigration, pay or grievance details to managers. Client contacts and PRO/provider interviews supplement rather than replace customer evidence.

## Recruitment channels and message

Proposed channels: founder introductions, accounting/payroll practitioners, agency associations and professional communities, sector operators and targeted LinkedIn outreach. These are channel hypotheses; no access or partnership exists yet. Include non-founder contacts to reduce network bias.

Suggested invitation, for founder use only:

> We are studying how UAE employers and recruitment teams coordinate hiring starts and payroll inputs. Could we discuss one recent real workflow for 30–45 minutes? We are researching the process, not selling a product. Please do not share employee personal documents or confidential client information.

No outreach is authorized or sent by this plan. Ask permission before recording; use redacted process artifacts or synthetic reconstructions. Store only necessary business contact details, restrict access, agree retention/deletion, and follow the participant's confidentiality rules.

## Interview script: start with the last event

**Context, 5 minutes:** What does the organization do? Which entities, jurisdictions, workforce types and countries are involved? Who owns hiring starts, payroll inputs and approvals? Which systems and providers are used, and who pays for them?

**Recent workflow, 15 minutes:**

1. Walk through the most recent completed placement/start or payroll cycle, from initiating event to final accepted output.
2. What happened at each handoff? Who entered, checked, approved and re-entered the data?
3. Show a redacted tracker, calendar, email sequence or screen if permitted. Which record was authoritative?
4. Where did the workflow depart from plan? How was it discovered? Who intervened?
5. How many comparable events occurred in the last two complete cycles? What is the denominator? Distinguish tracked counts from recollection.
6. Describe a successful cycle as well. What made the existing process sufficient?

**Consequences and alternatives, 10 minutes:**

7. What concrete cost, delay, adjustment, lost fee or extra work resulted? Which part was recoverable? Can a redacted record support it?
8. Which causes were internal coordination, client delay, worker choice, government processing, missing capacity, or something else? Which were preventable by software?
9. What have you tried? Show current ATS/HRMS configuration, partner service or spreadsheet workaround. Why was it retained or abandoned?
10. What does that alternative cost, including internal time? What would switching or adding a system require?

**Buying and constraints, 10 minutes:**

11. Who can approve spending, and how did your last similar purchase happen? What competing priorities receive that budget?
12. What integrations, data permissions, security checks and external approvals are mandatory before evaluation?
13. What outcome would justify a bounded, paid workflow evaluation? Who must participate, and what would stop it?
14. Who else disagrees about this problem, or considers it solved? Can we interview them?

Show the concept only after documenting the existing process. Never ask “Would you pay for AI HR software?” as primary validation. Ask for a concrete next action: a second stakeholder meeting, a redacted baseline, or discussion of a paid evaluation with the budget owner. A positive answer is still not a purchase.

## Workflow-specific probes

| Workflow | Evidence to seek | Disconfirming signal |
|---|---|---|
| Placement-to-start | Planned vs actual starts; accepted-offer cohort; fee event; candidate/client ownership; last failed handoff | Slippage mainly reflects decisions or processing times beyond coordination control; ATS reminders already suffice |
| Payroll inputs | Cutoff calendar; source systems; approval histories; late changes; bureau rejection and correction logs | Clean inputs already flow automatically; main defect is calculation or bank/provider execution outside the proposed scope |
| International document readiness | Who requests/checks/submits each item; authorized provider's role; expiry/escalation history | A trusted provider already owns the process end to end; extra portal creates duplicate work |
| Staffing time-to-invoice | Approved time, assignment changes, invoice acceptance and disputes | Problem requires a full back-office replacement or custom integration before any independent value |

## Observation and quantitative baseline

Follow one accepted-placement cohort and one payroll cycle at willing organizations. Record the start/end definitions before collecting data. Use minimum necessary redacted fields: organization ID, event ID, planned/actual milestone dates, owner role, exception category, follow-up count, estimated effort and evidence quality. Keep worker names, passport numbers, health records and actual pay data out of the research log.

Measure median and range within each organization, not a misleading pooled average. Separate accepted offers from job openings, active workers from internal staff, and hours saved from elapsed processing time. Follow cohorts to closure; identify unresolved cases and canceled placements. Self-reported memory is weaker than timestamped records.

## Synthesis and progression gates

Use the evidence ledger in [PROBLEM_VALIDATION.md](PROBLEM_VALIDATION.md). Have the researcher record a contradictory interpretation before scoring each opportunity. Compare organization-level counts, observed severity, preventability, alternative adequacy and purchasing evidence.

**[E] Advance to a bounded validation engagement only when:** at least five independent organizations in one narrow segment show a recent recurring problem; at least three provide corroborating artifacts or observed workflows; at least two economic buyers agree to a specific paid evaluation with success criteria; and an incumbent configuration test fails to solve the same problem cheaply. These are decision heuristics, not proof of market size or a statistical test.

Do not build features during Phase 0. Any later paid evaluation needs separate scope, data-processing terms, legal boundaries, and explicit authorization. Require actual paid commitments and measurable outcomes before treating willingness to pay as validated.
