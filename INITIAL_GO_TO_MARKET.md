# Initial go-to-market plan

Prepared: 1 November 2026. **Proposed experiments only; no campaigns, partnerships, sales, or product launches have occurred.** All targets and economics below are **[E] assumptions**. Customer needs and positioning are **[H]** pending the [interview plan](CUSTOMER_INTERVIEW_PLAN.md).

## Initial focus and positioning

Test UAE permanent-placement agencies with existing ATS systems and recurring accepted offers. Target the owner or operations head as the economic buyer, with recruiters/account managers as users and client HR/candidates as external participants. Confirm who owns start coordination before qualifying an account.

Proposed message for later validation:

> Keep accepted placements moving toward their agreed start date, with clear ownership of outstanding tasks across your team and client contacts.

This is a proposed value proposition, not a performance claim. Do not promise fewer delays, revenue recovery, legal compliance or integration compatibility until demonstrated. Avoid an “all-in-one UAE HR platform” message before a narrow job is validated.

The alternative message for payroll-input discovery is: “Resolve missing and disputed payroll inputs before the agreed cutoff, with an approval trail for your existing provider.” Test messages against the recipient's last actual incident, not click-through alone.

## Partner model and diligence

Treat ATS implementers, payroll providers, PRO/mobility firms and sector consultants as candidate partners, not established relationships. Distinguish referral, implementation, reseller and data-integration arrangements. Ask who owns support, localization, liability, customer relationship, data processing and termination/export.

Before agreeing terms, test one joint customer workflow and compare customer acquisition/support costs with direct sales. Referral fees, revenue shares and exclusivity are unknown; do not assume standard rates. Avoid exchanging employee documents as a referral mechanism or requiring a partner's regulated license to be treated as the software company's license.

For B, bank/exchange or payroll-provider cooperation may be valuable, but no WPS certification, government endorsement, payment integration or partnership is assumed. For A, API access and marketplace approval are vendor-specific prerequisites, not guaranteed routes to market.

## Pricing discovery and willingness to pay

No validated price or budget range exists. Compare three possible billing models with economic buyers:

- Agency subscription tied to an agreed active-placement band, which may align with operational volume but creates seasonality and counting questions.
- Internal-user subscription, which is familiar in ATS comparisons but may discourage cross-team participation.
- Flat organization fee with bounded scope, which is easier to explain but may poorly reflect very different volumes and service needs.

Ask which entity pays, how budgets are approved, what current service/software cost is displaced, and whether client/candidate access must be free to encourage participation. Do not call any model optimal until tested. Avoid a placement-success fee initially: it can create attribution disputes and role confusion; confirm legal and commercial boundaries before considering it.

Anchor a later paid-evaluation proposal to documented preventable cost and delivery scope, not invented competitor pricing. Record offered price, accepted/rejected terms, decision role and reason. A survey price preference, nonbinding letter or unpaid trial does not establish willingness to pay.

## Sales and onboarding motion to test after Phase 0

1. Qualify recurring workflow, named owner and current alternative.
2. Review a recent redacted incident and quantify preventable impact with the customer.
3. Demonstrate whether native ATS configuration already resolves it. Stop if it does.
4. Agree a bounded evaluation scope, outcome definitions, participant access, responsibilities, data terms and paid proposal.
5. Only after build/evaluation authorization, establish a baseline and track one complete cohort or cycle to closure.
6. Review net value including internal effort and vendor support; ask for a paid continuation only when outcomes justify it.

No paid evaluation is currently offered, contracted or implemented. Do not imply integrations, features or customer results exist. Phase 0 ends with a decision and evidence, not a software launch.

## Metrics and economic discipline

Track at organization level: qualified accounts, invitations, responses, interviews, artifact-backed pain, buyer meetings, paid commitments, collected revenue, time to decision, onboarding effort, active workflow completion, support effort and continuation. Separate conditional conversion at each stage and report small denominators.

For later operation, calculate:

`CAC = attributable acquisition cash costs + sales labor valued consistently, divided by new paying organizations`

`Monthly gross profit per account = recognized recurring revenue − directly attributable delivery, support and infrastructure costs`

`Simple CAC payback months = CAC / monthly gross profit per account` only when the denominator is positive and the measurement period and retention assumptions are disclosed.

Do not report LTV from a few early accounts or treat founder time as free. Track bespoke implementation and partner commissions separately. No target CAC, margin, payback or revenue is claimed without real cost and sales data.

## Four-week discovery sequence [E]

| Period | Work | Decision output |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Obtain source access; capture current official regulations/vendor plans; recruit first interviews | Verified source ledger and corrected scope assumptions |
| Week 2 | Observe agency handoffs and employer payroll inputs; include satisfied incumbent users | Corroborated incidents, successful counterexamples and loss mechanisms |
| Week 3 | Complete comparison interviews, native-tool tests, provider and buyer conversations | Evidence-adjusted scorecard and realistic implementation boundaries |
| Week 4 | Discuss specific paid evaluation terms with qualified buyers; synthesize contradictions | Advance one wedge, narrow the segment, or stop; no feature build in this phase |

Sequence depends on access and recruitment; these are not promised completion dates. Regulatory/source retrieval and interviews can proceed independently when available.

## Expansion and final recommendation

If A achieves repeatable paid value, test adjacent agency types or one additional market at a time. Do not add temporary staffing payroll/invoicing or multiple-country rules merely because the same customer asks. They may be different products with different economics and obligations.

Recommend **agency placement-to-start coordination as the first discovery campaign**, with payroll-input exception management as challenger. The rationale is a bounded workflow near a commercial event and a possible addition beside an existing system. The evidence remains weak: there are no customer interviews, current verified prices, hands-on competitor tests or paid commitments. Network access or supplied sources, followed by the planned customer evidence, is required before a build recommendation is defensible.
